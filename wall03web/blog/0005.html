learning to love my enemy

This story takes place during my high school years.

Introduction

My parents raised me a Quaker. I was taught to love everyone and give everyone a chance. Of course, that's harder to do in practice.

While I'm not great at being nice to everyone, I try my best. However, when I have a grudge against someone (no matter the reason) it's hard to back off

My enemy

I started freshmen year with an already-established friend group. I had a couple of friends I hung out with during lunch and whom I had my classes with all day. High school introduced some new people, including this one kid named Sam (name changed for privacy reasons)

Sam made some big strides. He wanted to be included and a part of the friend group. I totally understand that. It is hard to go to a new school, but the problem was that my friend group had built upon chemistry and inside jokes for years that he had to try to integrate into.

We didn't want to invite him to all birthday parties and events right away, even thought he was pushing into our group. I totally give him credit for trying--I would have just given up. The group should have given him stronger signals, but we just kinda ignored him

Learning to love

We had to take a sport during winter season in freshmen year, so I took swimming. It just so happened that the only other person at around my skill level in swimming was Sam. We had a lane together. It took a while to admit, but being forced to hang out together for an hour every day for three months really brought us together

While I didn't invite him to my birthday party, it doesn't feel like a chore when he is at our table during lunch. I applied these lessons to other people around my life. While I wasn't perfect (and still am not) it isn't hard to see all the effort I have put in.
